What happened

A routing change shipped. The average looked fine. Four people paid for it.

7 Apr · 4.0Nav, profile redesign, override audit log
16 Jun · 4.1Travel-time proximity, bulk callout
12 Aug · 4.2Proximity weight 0.45 → 0.60. Ping wait 90s → 60s.
12–14 AugBurst of missed pings (48% missed on 13 Aug)
17 Aug →“Phone never goes off” tickets begin; peak week of 24 Aug (32)

In plain words

4.2 shifted routing toward nearby responders and shortened how long a ping waits. A missed ping costs a responder the same as turning one down (−0.12). The only way a score goes up is taking a ping — but a low score means you stop being pinged. For four responders, a bad few days on 12–14 Aug became a trap with no exit.

The overall acceptance rate recovered (54% → 73% taken) so the headline metric hid the damage.

What the two piles of feedback said

~1/3 of tickets: “gone before I could answer” — explained by the 60s wait, starts 12 Aug.

~2/3 of tickets: “phone never goes off” — starts 17 Aug, tracks the quiet four’s volume drop.

No success metric or baseline was ever set for 4.2, so nobody was watching for this.

Six modules · one finding and one prompt each

From my prompt to the evidence

1
Origin Story · finding

4.2 had a purpose but no way to tell if it worked

The release notes and Priya’s handoff frame 4.2 as fairness to nearby and wide-area responders. No success metric or baseline was ever set.

Super Hearing · finding

Before 4.2 the four were normal; then they missed 55%

They had 11–14 pings/wk, 69–81% taken, no miss run over 1. In 12–16 Aug they missed 17 of 31 pings versus 21% for the others. Three had 3+ misses in a row on 13–14 Aug.

please check if these 4 had a series of misses before they went quiet
3
Rewind · finding

Their pings fell 49 → 3 a week while the other 12 held

Combined weekly pings: 49, 43, 16, 6, 3. Share of all pings 28% → 2%. Farlight hit zero by 31 Aug. After 4.2 other responders started getting pings for the quiet four’s areas.

how many pings were the quiet four getting before and after 4.2
4
X-Ray Vision · finding

The code has no way back up except taking a ping

Score runs 0–1, starts at 0.5. Take +0.08, miss or decline −0.12, no decay, no reset, no override. A floor-score responder sits ~9 minutes of travel time behind a neutral one (≈20 before 4.2).

does the code have anything around how a responder can recover if they've dropped to the bottom
5
Super Speed · artifact

A one-page brief and a clickable prototype

The brief proposes a comeback ping, a lighter penalty for a miss than a turn-down, and a read-only quiet marker for the handler. The prototype lets you click through Kip’s console and Meteor Mite’s phone. Saved as 05-super-speed/brief.md and prototype.html.

The note says this shouldn't just be a setting we quietly flip. Using Kip and Meteor Mite, walk me through what he'd actually see change, next time she goes quiet.

Sidekicks · artifact

review-checklist scored the brief 2/4: no named owner (“Dispatch PM”) and “Next” didn’t cover the smaller touches, the Supply warning or the comeback-ping trigger.

Why it happened — working hypothesis

A trap with no exit

From 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing (4.2 state). Every step below is in the code except where marked.

Miss ×3 · −0.36→ Score sinks→ Stops being pinged→ No chance to “take”→ +0.08 never earned

Only two code changes shipped in 4.2, both in config.py: ping wait 90s → 60s, and weights 0.45/0.40/0.15 → 0.60/0.25/0.15. A miss and a turn-down cost the same. The ranking can’t tell “my phone was in a lift” from “I said no”.

Not proven. Stormwrack also missed 3 in a row on 12–13 Aug and stayed busy, so a streak alone doesn’t sink anyone. Scores aren’t in the database, so the real rank scores for 12–17 Aug are unconfirmed. Seasonality can’t be ruled out without last year’s August data. Wen Li’s answers are still outstanding.
The proposed fix — shown, not described

Give a quiet responder a way back, and tell the handler why

Today

Kip sees no alert, no reason. Meteor Mite’s phone stays quiet. He finds out when work dries up.

With the fix

Console shows a quiet marker with the cause in plain words. Kip clicks Check availability; Meteor Mite gets one comeback ping and a 60-second clock. A take clears the marker. A miss asks her why. No boost button, no handler dial.

Try it: on Kip’s console click Check availability, switch to Meteor Mite’s phone, then take it, turn it down, or let the 60s clock run out. Colours: green active, yellow quiet, red not available. Fictional data. If the frame is blank, open the prototype directly.

Not in the fix

No revert of 4.2 · no handler boost · no change to the ping wait · Supply warned before launch.

Success looks like

Quiet four back above ~8 pings/wk · callouts with no taken ping back to ~5.5% (11.1% now) · overall taken not worse than 73%.

The prototype sends the comeback ping when Kip clicks, but the brief has the system send it automatically. That trigger conflict is undecided. The 5-day trigger and 0.14 starting score are placeholders. Not yet agreed with Wen Li, Sofia or Marcus.
